# visual-language — what a look must be before it is built

A direction is a set of decisions specific to **this** product and **this** audience, or it is the
generic answer wearing their colours. Everything here is judged against one question: would this have
come out the same for any other brief? If yes, it was not designed.

## What the direction is made of

- **Name the subject, the audience and the surface's single job before deciding anything.** Not pinned
  down, pin it yourself and say what you picked; a direction decided against nobody in particular is
  where every generic choice comes from.
- **A direction is four things, and it is written as four things: colour, type, layout, signature.**
  Four to six named hex values. Two or more type roles — a characterful display used with restraint, a
  body face, a utility face where data or captions need one. A layout concept in a sentence. And one
  element this product is remembered by.
- **Check the four against the brief before writing a line.** Work through what you would produce for
  any similar brief; wherever you arrive at the same place, that part is a default and gets revised.
  Say what you changed.
- **Typography carries the personality, not the content.** Pair display and body deliberately, set a
  scale with intentional weights and spacing, and make the type itself memorable rather than a neutral
  delivery vehicle.
- **Structure encodes meaning or it is removed.** Numbering, eyebrows, dividers and labels are only
  earned where the content genuinely is a sequence, a category, an aside. Decoration that mimics
  structure reads as structure and lies.
- **Match the execution to the ambition.** Maximalist directions need elaborate follow-through;
  minimal ones need precision in spacing, type and detail. Elegance is executing the chosen thing well.

## The defaults that give a machine away

- **Three looks arrive by themselves and are never a choice: cream and high-contrast serif with a
  terracotta accent; near-black with one acid-green or vermilion accent; broadsheet hairlines, zero
  radius, dense columns.** Each is legitimate when the brief asks for it. Where the brief leaves an
  axis free, that freedom is never spent on one of these.
- **Motion is orchestrated or it is cut.** One considered moment — a load sequence, a scroll reveal, a
  hover that says what happened — lands harder than effects scattered everywhere, and scattered effects
  are the clearest tell that nobody chose them.

## Where the boldness goes

- **Spend it in one place.** The signature element is the memorable thing; everything around it stays
  quiet and disciplined. Cut what does not serve the brief — and look once more before shipping to
  remove one thing that survived only because it was already there.
- **Taking no risk is its own risk.** A direction nobody could object to is a direction nobody
  remembers.
- **Critique while you build, on the thing itself.** Look at what is actually rendering, not at what
  you intended; a screenshot answers in a second what a paragraph of reasoning cannot.

## The floor nothing ships below

- **Responsive to mobile, keyboard focus visible, reduced motion respected.** Built in, never announced,
  and never a later pass.
- **Every state, not the good one.** Empty, loading, broken, overflowing. A surface with perfect content
  is the one case that designs itself.
