# Answer the warden — a question needs a reply, a flag needs a stop

A warden keeps the board in this project. It never tells you how to work and has no opinion on what
you build: all it sends is a question naming a card and a rule that does not appear to hold, and, when
that goes unanswered, a flag. Treated as one more opinion among your own, the keeper is worth nothing —
its entire value is that a question actually gets answered and a flag actually stops something.

- **Answer the question before the card moves again.** One sentence is enough, and most questions end
  there: the keeper cannot see what you know. Ignoring it is what turns it into a flag.
- **Stop the flagged card at the next safe point.** Finish the tool call in flight, then stop that
  card's work — never the session, never anything else you are carrying.
- **Everything independent keeps running.** Work not depending on the flagged card is not blocked by
  it, and idling everything over one question is its own failure.
- **Believing it is wrong is fine; acting on that belief is not.** Say so back to the keeper and wait.
  Deciding it is mistaken and carrying on is the one move that makes the watch a lie — as is routing
  the work around it by renaming the card, splitting it, or handing it to a subagent.
- **Resume only on something that settles it** — the keeper's word, or the human's. Silence is not
  consent.
- **The human at the keyboard outranks any flag.** Told directly to carry on, carry on, and say so to
  the keeper so it learns where that line is.
