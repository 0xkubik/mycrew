# Keep the board current — a card says what is true right now, or it says nothing

A board earns its place by being faster to trust than asking. It loses that in a day: a card sits in
progress with nobody on it, another is closed with half its criteria unticked, a third was corrected by
hand and quietly lost its history. None of it looks broken from the outside, and each one teaches
whoever reads it next to go and ask a person instead — at which point the board is pure overhead.

- **Never edit a card by hand — every change goes through the board's own tool.** Its sections are
  delimited by markers that tool writes and reads; a hand-edited card loses its relationships and its
  history, and nothing warns anyone that it did.
- **In progress means someone is holding it at this moment.** A card in progress that nobody is working
  is the worst thing a board can say, because the work looks owned and is not — moving it back costs a
  keystroke and buys back the board's whole credibility.
- **A blocked card names what blocks it, and the blocker exists as a card too.** Blocked and silent is
  indistinguishable from abandoned, and gets treated as abandoned soon enough.
- **A card closes only when every criterion on it is ticked.** Where the work could sprawl, its boundary
  is set before the work starts — otherwise the work sets its own and never reaches it.
- **The board is neither the plane nor a report.** What the product must be is written elsewhere and is
  never invented on a card; work that is done is closed, not narrated for a reader who will never come.
- **None of this reaches work kept off the board.** Scratch in someone's own session is theirs — but the
  moment a piece of work is on the board, it is claiming to be true, and it answers for that.
